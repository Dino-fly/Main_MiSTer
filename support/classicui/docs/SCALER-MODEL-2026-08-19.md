# The scaler model against the silicon, 2026-08-19

`vp_render_exact()` in `chome_video.cpp` and `tools/simulate_look.py` are two
implementations of the MiSTer scaler's arithmetic, both derived by reading
`ascal.vhd` and `shadowmask.sv`, and pinned to each other by
`assert_scaler_matches_model()`. Until today neither had ever been compared to the
hardware. Two implementations agreeing proves consistency, not correctness — a
misreading of the RTL would sit in both, and the harness would agree with it for
ever.

`DEVELOPMENT.md` §3 listed the scaler as something a green harness says nothing
about, on the grounds that `screenshot` captures core video *pre*-scaler and the
framebuffer never carries composited output. **A USB HDMI capture device breaks
that assumption**, because it sees the transmitter's output — after the polyphase
filter, the mask and the gamma. One arrived, so this is the comparison.

`simulate_look.py`'s own docstring asked for it: *"calibrate against a real
capture when the HDMI grabber arrives; the knobs exist so calibration is a flag,
not a rewrite."*

---

## The rig, and why its numbers can be trusted

- **Capture**: UGREEN UVC device (`0x2b89:0x5389`), `ffmpeg -f avfoundation`,
  raw `uyvy422` straight to disk. **Luma is read from the raw bytes**, so
  ffmpeg's YUV→RGB conversion is not in the loop and 4:2:2 chroma subsampling
  cannot contaminate the measurement.
- **Source**: `screenshot` over `/dev/MiSTer_cmd`, which is the core's frame
  *before* the scaler — exactly the input the model takes.
- **Subject**: the GBA core with **no cartridge**, whose "GAME BOY" boot logo is
  static and has hard high-contrast edges in every orientation. Two captures a
  second apart were **byte-identical** — mean |diff| 0.000, max 0. There is no
  capture noise to work above.
- **Colour**: fitted against the source. **BT.709, studio range** (16–235) is the
  transmitter's output: mean |error| 0.667 with 91.0% of samples exact, against
  BT.601's 1.247. Full range is not close (mean 19). That 0.667 is the noise
  floor every number below is measured against.
- **Control**: with **no filter loaded**, the hardware output is *exact 3×
  nearest-neighbour* of the source — 91% of luma samples exact. So the geometry
  maps 1:1, there is no hidden resampling, and any residual under a filter is the
  filter.

Scales tested, both genuinely integer and both confirmed from the scaler's own
header:

| mode | source | scaler output | scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720×480 | 240×160 | 720×480 | **3× both axes** |
| 1920×1080 | 240×160 | 1440×960 | **6× both axes** |

Look under test: **GBA** (`agb001`) — `F_GRID` on both axes, **no shadow mask, no
scanline filter, no gamma**. That is the one shape `simulate_look.py` claims to
model exactly; the scanline vfilter and adaptive filters are declared unmodelled,
so any look using them would have confounded the result. The model was fed the
*actual generated filter file* off the card (`filters/ClassicHome LCD Grid.txt`),
regenerated by the front-end for each magnification.

---

## Result 1 — the geometry is exact

At both scales, in flat white, the grid's period and phase match the hardware
**exactly**: the dark gutter falls on sub-pixel 0 of every source pixel, on both
axes, in both the capture and the model.

This is the property that matters most and the one with a scar behind it — a grid
narrower than 256/N does nothing at magnification N, and this front-end has
shipped a build where it vanished at exactly 4× while working at 5×. At 3× and 6×
the real hardware puts the gutter where the model says it will.

Against the null hypothesis — "no filter at all" — the model is **4.8× closer**
(mean 7.1 vs 33.9 at 3×). The filter is unquestionably being reproduced.

## Result 2 — the photometry is approximate, and the error changes sign

Flat white source (`0xF7`), one cell of the repeating pattern, luma:

| scale | axis | hardware | model |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3× | horizontal | 196, 194, 194, 197 | 198.9 (constant) |
| 3× | vertical | 175, 171, 171, 177 | 178.3 (constant) |
| 6× | horizontal | 170, 171 | 158.6 |
| 6× | vertical | 165, 165 | 152.6 |

Three things fall out of that:

1. **At 3× the model's gutter is ~3 levels too light; at 6× it is ~12 levels too
   dark.** The sign flips with magnification, so this is not a constant offset
   that could be tuned away.
2. **The hardware's gutter varies cell to cell** (194/196/197) where the model is
   perfectly constant. That is the source-position accumulator's fraction — at
   3× the step is 256/3 = 85.33, which never lands twice in the same place.
3. **At 6× the hardware's "bright" pixels are 229–234, not 235.** The real gutter
   bleeds into its neighbours; the model draws a hard single-pixel notch.

Whole-frame means, luma, against a 0.667 noise floor:

| scale | model, best alignment | model, phase-bias 0 | no filter (null) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3× | **5.11** | 7.13 | 33.90 |
| 6× | **4.42** | 4.42 | — |

Where the residual lives, at 3×: **4.2 on flat areas, 17.9 within one pixel of an
edge**, and by sub-phase 7.9 / 4.3 / 3.1 — concentrated on the gutter pixel. It
does **not** drift across the frame (4.1 / 5.6 / 5.6 / 4.1 by eighths), so it is
not an accumulator that runs away; it is the tap arithmetic.

## Result 3 — `--phase-bias` cannot be calibrated once

The knob the docstring asked to have calibrated has a **different optimum at each
magnification**:

- **3×**: a sharp minimum at bias **222–224** (mean 5.11), with 36+ either side.
- **6×**: the minimum is at **0** (mean 4.42), flat over 0–16, with 31+ elsewhere.

Both are well-defined optima, and 223 is not congruent to 0 under the 256/N phase
period. So the initial phase is not one hardware constant that the model is
missing — it varies with the ratio, and a single `--phase-bias` value cannot be
"the" calibration. The flag is still useful for authoring a lookshot at a known
scale; it is not the correction the docstring hoped for.

## What this does and does not change

**It does not affect anything that ships.** The real filters run in the FPGA and
are unaffected by any of this. What the model drives is the Display screen's
preview tiles and the authored lookshots — illustrations, and documented as such
in `chome_video.h` ("the content is real, the effect is simulated").

**It does put a number on how good the illustration is**, which nobody could
before: structurally faithful, photometrically within about 3–12 luma levels on
the gutter, and least accurate where the filter's taps span an edge. A player
choosing between looks is not misled. A developer using the model to judge
whether a filter is *correct* would be, at the margin.

**It narrows `DEVELOPMENT.md` §3.** "The harness holds a software model, not the
silicon" is still true, but the model is no longer unfalsifiable: this comparison
can be re-run in about ten minutes whenever the filter generation changes.

## Open, and worth someone's time

- **The gutter depth.** Both the sign flip and the neighbour bleed point at the
  tap weighting or the truncation points rather than at alignment. The model
  documents two separate truncations (`poly_final` taking bits 26:8, then the
  slice add); that is the first place to look.
- **An observation I could not close.** After `video_mode 8` changed the
  magnification from 3× to 6×, `filters/ClassicHome LCD Grid.txt` still held the
  3× grid until the in-game menu was next opened, ~15 seconds later.
  `vp_output_poll()` exists to catch exactly that and carries a comment about the
  4× case, so this may be a gap in when it runs or may be my reading of the log.
  Stated as an observation, not a diagnosis.
- **Other looks.** Only the pure-polyphase grid was tested. The shadow mask is
  modelled and untested against hardware; the scanline vfilter is not modelled at
  all and is used by every CRT look.

## Reproducing it

1. GBA core, no cartridge (static logo, hard edges, zero capture noise).
2. `echo "video_mode 2" > /dev/MiSTer_cmd` for 3×, `video_mode 8` for 6×.
3. Apply the GBA look from the Display screen; confirm the log names
   `Filter 'ClassicHome LCD Grid.txt'` on both axes.
4. `screenshot` for the source; copy `filters/ClassicHome LCD Grid.txt` off the card.
5. `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -pixel_format uyvy422 -video_size WxH -i "0" -frames:v 1
   -f rawvideo -pix_fmt uyvy422 out.uyvy`
6. `simulate_look.py src.png pred.png <scale> --filter grid.txt [--phase-bias N]`
7. Compare **luma** — byte `2n+1` of the UYVY stream — against
   `16 + (0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B) × 219/255`.
